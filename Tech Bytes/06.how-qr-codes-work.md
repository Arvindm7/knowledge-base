# How QR Codes Work (And Why They Still Scan When Damaged)

> A QR code is a 2D matrix barcode with built-in orientation markers and mathematical error correction — letting scanners read it upside down, at an angle, or even with up to 30% of it covered by a logo.

## What You'll Learn

- What each visual pattern on a QR code actually does
- How QR codes encode text, URLs, and binary data
- How Reed-Solomon error correction lets damaged QR codes still scan
- Why companies can put logos right in the middle of a QR code
- Security tips before scanning unknown QR codes

---

## Why QR Codes Were Invented

QR stands for **Quick Response**. It was invented in **1994** by Masahiro Hara at **Denso Wave** (a Toyota subsidiary in Japan) to track car parts on factory assembly lines. Traditional 1D barcodes could only hold ~20 characters and had to be scanned straight-on; QR codes solved both problems by storing data **horizontally and vertically**.

---

## Anatomy of a QR Code

Every QR code is made of tiny black and white squares called **modules**. Not all of them hold your data — many are structural landmarks that help a camera decode it in milliseconds:

| Pattern | Where It Lives | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Finder Patterns** | Three large squares in the top-left, top-right, and bottom-left corners | Tell the scanner *"this is a QR code"* and instantly reveal its **rotation** (since the 4th corner has no finder square) |
| **Alignment Patterns** | Smaller squares inside larger QR codes | Correct for **perspective distortion** when the code is printed on a curved bottle or scanned at an angle |
| **Timing Patterns** | Alternating black/white dotted lines connecting the finder squares | Help the scanner measure the exact grid size (how many rows and columns) |
| **Format & Version Info** | Strips next to the finder patterns | Declare the error-correction level and mask pattern used |
| **Quiet Zone** | Blank white border around the outside | Separates the QR code from surrounding visual clutter |
| **Data & Error Correction** | The remaining area (read in a zig-zag pattern from bottom-right) | Stores the actual encoded message plus redundancy bytes |

---

## Masking: Why QR Codes Look "Random"

If a QR code accidentally had a large solid black block or a pattern that looked like a fake finder square, scanners could get confused.

To prevent this, the QR encoder tries **8 different XOR mask patterns** (like checkerboards or stripes) over the data area and picks the one that produces the most balanced, easy-to-scan mix of black and white squares.

---

## Error Correction: The Real Magic

QR codes use **Reed-Solomon error correction** — the same family of math used to recover scratched CDs, DVDs, and deep-space Voyager transmissions.

When generating a QR code, you choose one of **four error correction levels**:

| Level | Recoverable Damage | Typical Use Case |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **L (Low)** | ~7% | Clean digital screens, maximum data density |
| **M (Medium)** | ~15% | General everyday use (default in most generators) |
| **Q (Quartile)** | ~25% | Printed flyers, industrial labels |
| **H (High)** | ~30% | Outdoor posters, **QR codes with a logo in the center** |

### Why Logos in the Middle Still Work

When you see a brand logo sitting right in the center of a QR code, the logo isn't "part of the code" — it is literally **damaging/covering** those data modules! Because the code was generated at **Level H**, Reed-Solomon math reconstructs the missing bytes automatically.

---

## How Much Data Can Fit?

QR codes come in **Version 1** (`21 × 21` modules) up to **Version 40** (`177 × 177` modules). A Version 40 QR code at Level L can store:

- Up to **7,089** numeric digits, OR
- Up to **4,296** alphanumeric characters, OR
- Up to **2,953** bytes of binary data (~3 KB)

In practice, smaller versions scan much faster, which is why URL shorteners are often paired with printed QR codes.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Quishing (QR Phishing):** Because humans can't read a QR code with the naked eye, attackers sometimes stick fake QR stickers over legitimate parking meters or restaurant menus. Always preview the URL before tapping to open it.

## Summary

| Feature | How It Helps |
| :--- | :--- |
| **3 Finder Squares** | Instant detection and 360° orientation |
| **Alignment & Timing** | Accurate grid reading even on tilted or curved surfaces |
| **Mask Patterns** | Prevents confusing solid blocks or false finder patterns |
| **Reed-Solomon (L/M/Q/H)** | Recovers 7%–30% missing/damaged area (enabling center logos) |
